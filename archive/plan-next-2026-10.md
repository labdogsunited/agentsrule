1. **`emerge-ref` speed work** (`cpu-engineering-insights.md`, `hardware-opportunities.md`; do it when no batch is running, so timings are clean). Start with two cheap checks: 16 runs pinned one per physical core vs 32 runs (completed ticks per hour, fixed seeds), and a `target-cpu=native` build kept beside the normal one (results must match exactly). **Done 2026-10-18** (32 jobs: `test9p` dish, seeds 1–32, 1_000 ticks; all outputs identical apart from `ms_per_tick`): 32 at once 485 s, 16 at once 435 s, 16 pinned one per core 435 s, 16 with `target-cpu=native` 438 s. So run 16 at once (about 10% more work per hour, and each run finishes sooner); pinning and the native build give nothing, so keep the portable build. Then reusable scratch buffers in the mind step, then a per-entity list of active genes, then a nearest-only contact query; structure-of-arrays only if a profile points to memory layout. Rules for every change:
   - **Children come from the inherited genome, never from an execution plan** (`evolutionary-semantics.md` §6). Dormant genes, module boundaries, signal wiring and gene order stay in the genome even if execution skips them; a plan is rebuilt from the genome.
   - Keep gene order, fee order, tie rules and random-draw coordinates.
   - Check each change against the old binary on the full state (`--dump`, plus signals and genomes) and on offspring from `emerge census`, not only on the CSV.
   - **Findings 2026-10-19 (`~/perf2/`, `Builder` with mutation on in the guard world, 40_000–49_000 entities).** One run alone: 39 s for 600 ticks; 16 copies at once: 209 s each, so batch runs are about 5× slower than one run alone and are limited by memory traffic, not instructions (one run alone: IPC 1.5). Phase times of one run: set 31%, transfer arbitration 32%, upkeep, fees and building requests 16%, transform 13%, movement and restructure 8%. About 1.3 million of the ~1.6 million requests per tick are world rules (inflow and diffusion: 4 + 16 per cell). A cached list of active genes gave identical output but no gain (139 → 140 s alone, 209 → 210 s at 16 copies), so it was reverted: single-run profiles mislead here. Yardstick for every change: the 16-copy time. Deferred 2026-10-19 (the user chose experiment tooling first): a dense path for world requests (exactness hazards: diffusion shares origin slots with `take`, destination headroom and overflow, draw order within groups); only keep it if it saves at least 20% at 16 copies. A throwaway bound (world rules applied without arbitration) changed the dynamics (67_000 against 40_000 entities), so it gave no clean upper bound. **Tried and discarded 2026-10-19:** a dense path for inflow and diffusion requests was exact (60 random small-grid worlds, 200 ticks each, with about 78_000 groups where diffusion competed with `take` and 157_000 where caps cut transfers; identical CSV, census and `--dump` on two real dishes) but not faster: 8 old against 8 new copies at once, three rounds, gave −3%, −5% and +1%. So request volume is not what limits batches. The patch and its differential test are kept outside the repo (`~/perf2/fast-path.patch`). Next speed lever, if any: measure what does limit them (memory per entity, for example) before writing code.

4. **Causal replay.** When an event of interest occurs, replay the winning lineage from the nearest checkpoint with instrumentation on, and write a causal story. The forward run stays lean. Write it as a proposal. **Proposal written 2026-10-19:** `causal-replay.md` (replay from the manifest with recording on, focal clade, story; first use: do producers in failed test 9 seeds appear and die, or never appear?).
   - **First replay (2026-10-19, `Drifter` seed 2, 22_000 ticks, binary before the review fixes, so no deaths recorded):** the main CSV matched the original run. Producers neither fail to appear nor get wiped out: 28 entities made body in 8 families, and body-making reactions spread far more widely than production does. Counting genes along each ancestry from the change log (gene presence only, expression ignored): of 191_952 births, 4_353 carried a body reaction and 2_712 a `take(nitrate)`, but only 117 both. The biggest family (#114463, 730 members) has a body reaction in 618 but `take(nitrate)` in 5; it made body once. The one family where the two pieces meet (#103597, 88 members, still growing at tick 22_000) has both in 48 and made 21 of the 28 producers. Every body reaction the producers inherited needs nitrate. So in this seed the bottleneck is joining the two pieces, not keeping producers alive. 8 producers had no nitrate source in their ancestry (probably stock inherited at division, or diff noise from the old binary); 3 got nitrate by breaking down body.
5. **Stepping stone diagnostic (2026-10-19, asked for after the first replay).** The question: if the body reaction is already in the genome, so that `take(nitrate)` pays as soon as mutation finds it, do producers establish? A dish-only stepping stone does not fit this chemistry. Energy comes only from E atoms, and nitrate has none, so nitrate cannot pay as food. Giving nitrate E would also open an easier body route (`nitrate + carbon -> heat + body`). So the test uses lineage `Halfway`: `Drifter` plus `reaction build rate 50mu` in `metabolism`, with no `take(nitrate)`. It is seeded with one piece of the route, so it is not test 9's unseeded condition. Dish: the guard dish (`~/test9guard/dish.em`, the same file as the seed 2 replay) with `Halfway` appended (`~/stepping/dish.em`). Flags as in the guard batch. Control: guard `Drifter`, 7 of 20.
   - **Screen first:** seeds 1 and 2 with `--features`. If `take(nitrate)` does not become common in them, record that and skip the batch.
   - **Stop rule, set before the batch:** one batch of seeds 1–20 (screen seeds included). 14 or more sustained: the joining of the two pieces is the main bottleneck. 8 or fewer: it is not; establishment is. 9–13: unclear. Record the result whatever it is, with no second batch.
6. **Unseeded stepping stone by catalysis (2026-10-19): screen failed, batch skipped.** `emerge-ref` now runs catalysts (spec §4.5, `bfc7119`). Dish: the guard dish with `respiration` catalysed by nitrate (`catalyze nitrate(half: 1u, boost: 4.0)`, `~/catalyst/dish.em`), so `take(nitrate)` could pay with genes `Drifter` already has, and no new body route opens. Screen: unseeded `Drifter`, seeds 1 and 2, 6_000 ticks, with and without the catalyst. `take(nitrate)` was active in 0.5% and 0.9% of entities with it, against 0.6% and 0.5% without; mean energy 85–86u in all four runs. Why: energy in this dish is limited by light, not by reaction speed. A 1_500-tick `--blocked` run shows `respiration` at its cap in 24% of gene-ticks and photosynthesis short of light in 32%, and 95% of `divide` intents lack body. A catalyst could pay only in a dish where reaction speed limits energy (more light, a lower `max_rate`), which is a recalibration of the whole dish. Not started.
   - **Result (2026-10-19): 20 of 20 sustained**, against 7 of 20 for guard `Drifter` and the rule of 14. Most seeds were sustained at ticks 10_000–11_000 (the earliest possible); the latest was at 37_000. So with the body reaction already present, `take(nitrate)` is found and producers establish in every seed: joining the two pieces is the main bottleneck, and establishment is not.
